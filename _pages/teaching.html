--- layout: archive title: "Teaching" permalink: /teaching/ author_profile: true --- ## Teaching Philosophy My favorite classrooms are ones where students are doing more than listening. I want students to ask questions, challenge assumptions, connect psychological science to their own experiences, and become comfortable changing their minds when the evidence calls for it. My teaching is guided by three goals: **fostering critical thinking, encouraging active engagement, and helping students connect psychology to their own lives.** Whether students are debating competing psychological theories, replicating a classic phenomenon with a friend, or analyzing a research article for the first time, I want them to experience psychology as something they can actively participate in—not simply a collection of findings to memorize. For example, in General Psychology, I have asked students to debate competing theories and then switch sides, challenging them to construct an argument for a position they initially disagreed with. For their final projects, students became the experimenters themselves, replicating classic psychological phenomena such as the Stroop effect, inattentional blindness, and primacy and recency effects with friends or family members. I also want students to see psychology in the world around them. Across my courses, I use everyday examples, videos, discussion, and students' own observations to connect psychological concepts to lived experience. My goal is for students to leave my classroom not only knowing more psychology, but also asking better questions about the people and world around them. --- ## Teaching Experience ### University of Connecticut **General Psychology (PSYC 1100)** *Instructor of Record, Fall 2022* 40 students **Language & Racism (COGS/AFRA 2345)** *Teaching Assistant & Guest Lecturer, Spring and Fall 2023* 35–40 students per semester Guest lectures included African American Vernacular English and a tutorial on Language Inquiry & Word Count (LIWC). **Developmental Psychology (PSYC 2400)** *Teaching Assistant & Guest Lecturer, Spring 2020 and Spring 2022* 150+ students per semester Guest lectures included attachment and moral development. **Principles of Research in Psychology (PSYC 2100)** *Lab Instructor, Fall 2021* 12 students Topics included data organization, basic statistics, and scientific writing. **General Psychology — Honors Lab (PSYC 1100)** *Lab Instructor, Fall 2020 and Spring 2021* 25 students per semester Topics included scientific literacy, basic statistics, and data visualization. **General Psychology (PSYC 1100)** *Lab Instructor, Fall 2019* 25 students Topics included scientific literacy, basic statistics, and data visualization. ### Eugene Lang College at The New School **Fundamentals of Developmental Psychology (LPSY 2036)** *Teaching Assistant, Spring 2019* 60 students --- ## Student Feedback Across my teaching roles, students have consistently highlighted the clarity, organization, accessibility, and interactive nature of my teaching. > *"She is very clearly talking about topics, and the way she teaches is like having a conversation with you, not just trying to teach you what is on the screen."* > — General Psychology student > *"I thought Cynthia Boo was an amazing instructor.
