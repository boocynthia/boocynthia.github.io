I study **the role of language in social development**. I'm fascinated by something we all do without thinking about it: using language to build relationships, read other people, and make sense of the social world. My research asks two broad questions: how do children adapt the way they talk depending on who they're talking to, and how does the language kids hear shape what they come to believe about other people? I answer these questions across neurotypical and neurodivergent development, mixing experimental, naturalistic, and computational methods from developmental psychology, cognitive science, and linguistics.

I'm currently a Postdoctoral Research Associate in the Department of Psychology at New York University (NYU), working with [Dr. Marjorie Rhodes](https://kidconcepts.org/about). I earned my Ph.D. in Psychological Sciences (Developmental Concentration) from the University of Connecticut (UConn), where I was advised by [Dr. Letty Naigles](https://cll.research.uconn.edu/). I also completed a [graduate certificate in Cognitive Sciences](https://cogsci.uconn.edu/graduate-certificate/) at UConn.

One line of my research looks at how children coordinate language with friends, including linguistic devices, such as backchannels ("mhm," "yeah"), discourse markers ("like," "well"), and linguistic alignment, and how these small moves relate to friendship quality. Another looks at neurodivergent communication, and specifically how differences in language use often say more about the demands of a given interaction than about diagnosis alone. A third examines generic language (e.g., *boys play with trucks*) and how hearing these broad, category-level statements shapes children's beliefs about gender and other social groups.

Outside of research, I care a lot about mentorship. I've supported undergraduate and graduate researchers through the full arc of a project, from a first literature review to presenting original work at conferences, and I love watching that process unfold. I also teach, having served as instructor of record for General Psychology and guest lecturer on topics from language and racism to developmental psychology.
