Much of my service has centered on **making academic spaces and opportunities more accessible**—whether that means helping students navigate an unfamiliar application process, creating spaces for members of an academic community to voice concerns, connecting educators with research on neurodiversity, or introducing young children to psychological science.

I am especially drawn to opportunities where I can use my own experiences and training to make academia a little easier to navigate for the people coming after me.

---

## Supporting Access & Mentorship

One of the most rewarding parts of my service has been helping students navigate academic pathways and application processes. Because many of the expectations surrounding higher education are implicit, I value opportunities to make those expectations more transparent and to help students identify paths that fit their own interests and goals.

### Application Support Group
**Mentor, New York University**  
*2026–Present*

Through NYU's Application Support Group, I mentor students from across the United States as they prepare applications for graduate programs and other academic opportunities. I work primarily with students on their **personal statements**, helping them identify the experiences and goals they want to communicate and develop those ideas into a clear narrative.

Rather than simply editing an application, I try to help students understand what a personal statement is doing, why certain information is useful to an admissions committee, and how they can communicate their experiences in their own voice.

### [MAGIC](https://magic.initiative.uconn.edu/)
**Mentor, University of Connecticut**  
*2022–2025*

Through UConn's MAGIC mentoring program, I worked with students from UConn and other institutions around the country who were considering their next steps in psychology and related fields.

My mentoring began with conversations about students' **research interests, longer-term goals, and what they were looking for in a graduate program**. From there, we identified programs that might be a good fit and worked together on application materials. I aimed to make the application process feel less daunting by breaking it into manageable decisions and giving students a space to ask questions about both the formal and informal aspects of applying to graduate school.

### Graduate School Access & Guest Speaking
**Guest Speaker & Panelist, University of Connecticut**  
*2020*

My own path through academia has not always followed a traditional trajectory, and I have valued opportunities to share those experiences with students who are beginning to consider their own paths.

I have spoken with students about **applying to graduate school generally, navigating the process as a first-generation student, and pursuing graduate training from an interdisciplinary Cognitive Science background**. In these conversations, I have tried to be transparent not only about how graduate applications work, but also about the uncertainty and decision-making that can accompany finding your place in academia.

Presentations included:

- **Applying to Graduate School as a First-Generation Student** — Guest Panelist, UConn Language Creation Lab
- **Applying to Graduate School** — Guest Presenter, UConn Language Creation Lab
- **Applying to Graduate School as a Cognitive Science Major** — Guest Presenter, UConn COGS 3584

## Diversity, Equity, Inclusion & Belonging

I have also contributed to efforts aimed at understanding and improving the climate of the academic communities in which I work. I see this kind of service as requiring both **creating opportunities for people to speak openly about their experiences and ensuring that those experiences are communicated back to the broader community**.

### Psychological Sciences Diversity & Inclusion Taskforce
**Taskforce Member, University of Connecticut**  
*2021–2024*

As a member of UConn Psychological Sciences' Diversity & Inclusion Taskforce, I contributed to departmental efforts to understand and improve the experiences of graduate students and faculty.

A major part of my work involved the department's **annual climate survey**. I helped summarize and disseminate findings from the survey so that patterns in community members' experiences could be communicated to the department rather than remaining simply as collected data.

I also helped organize and lead **open forums for members of the department to discuss concerns and grievances about departmental climate**. These forums created opportunities for members of the community to raise issues directly and for the taskforce to better understand concerns that might not be captured fully through a survey.

### SLAC Diversity, Equity, & Inclusion Committee
**Committee Member, University of Connecticut**  
*2021–2022*

I also served on the Diversity, Equity, & Inclusion Committee of UConn's student organization for language and cognition researchers. The committee met regularly to discuss issues related to **climate, inclusion, and the experiences of students within our academic community**.

This role complemented my departmental service by providing a smaller, student-centered space for conversations about how our immediate research community could be more inclusive and supportive.

## Neurodiversity & Inclusive Science

My interests in inclusion also intersect with my research on neurodivergent communication. In particular, I am interested in making research on neurodiversity useful and accessible to the educators and communities who interact with neurodivergent children in their everyday lives.

### Neurodiversity in Education: Building Educator-Researcher Partnerships
**Co-Facilitator, University of Connecticut**  
*2022*

I co-facilitated **Neurodiversity in Education: Building Educator-Researcher Partnerships**, an initiative designed to help educators connect with ongoing research on neurodiversity.

As part of the project, I helped create **accessible infographics translating research findings for educators**. The broader goal was to reduce the gap between research and educational practice by making current work on neurodiversity easier to find, understand, and engage with outside of academic journals.

## Community & Public Engagement

I also enjoy opportunities to communicate psychological science beyond traditional academic settings. These experiences have ranged from introducing school-aged children to scientific research to talking with undergraduate students about careers and identities in STEM.

### Women in STEM Frontiers Research Expo
**Guest Presenter, University of Connecticut**  
*2023*

I participated in UConn's **Women in STEM Frontiers Research Expo**, an outreach event designed to highlight and uplift women working in STEM fields. I shared my research and experiences as a psychological scientist, giving students an opportunity to see the kinds of questions that can be studied within psychological and developmental science.

### [Ask a Brain Scientist](https://database.outreach.engagement.uconn.edu/program/119)
**Instructor, University of Connecticut**  
*2022*

Through **Ask a Brain Scientist**, I worked with school-aged children to introduce them to psychological and brain science in an engaging and accessible way.

I especially enjoyed the opportunity to encourage children's curiosity and show them that science is not simply something they encounter in a textbook—it is a process of **asking questions about the world and figuring out how we might answer them**.

### Psi Chi Induction Ceremony
**Invited Speaker, University of Connecticut**  
*2023*

I was invited to speak to newly inducted members of UConn's Psi Chi chapter about my academic trajectory and experiences in psychology.

I used my own path through psychology—which has included several transitions across institutions, research areas, and stages of training—to talk with students about the many different ways an academic and professional trajectory can develop. Rather than presenting academic careers as following one predetermined path, I wanted students to see that interests and goals can evolve as they encounter new questions and opportunities.
