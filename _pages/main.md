I study **the role of language in children's social development**. I'm fascinated by how children use language to build relationships, understand other people, and make sense of the social world-often without realizing it. My research centers on two broad questions: How do children adapt the way they communicate depending on who they're talking to? And how does the language children hear shape what they come to believe about other people?

I'm currently a Postdoctoral Research Associate in the Department of Psychology at New York University (NYU), working with [Dr. Marjorie Rhodes](https://kidconcepts.org/about). I earned my Ph.D. in Psychological Sciences (Developmental Concentration) from the University of Connecticut (UConn), where I was advised by [Dr. Letty Naigles](https://cll.research.uconn.edu/). I also completed a [graduate certificate in Cognitive Sciences](https://cogsci.uconn.edu/graduate-certificate/) at UConn.

One line of my research asks *how children use language in social interactions*. I study how children coordinate their language with friends through behaviors such as backchannels (*mhm, yeah*), discourse markers (*like, well*), and linguistic alignment, and how these subtle features of conversation relate to friendship quality. I also study communication in neurodivergent populations, with a particular interest in how differences in language use can reflect the demands and dynamics of an interaction rather than diagnosis alone.

A second line of my research asks how language shapes children's social beliefs. Much of this work focuses on generic language (i.e., broad, category-level statements, such as *boys play with trucks*) and how hearing or using this kind of language influences children's beliefs about gender and other social groups. Across these areas, I combine experimental and naturalistic approaches with computational methods to study language and social cognition in both neurotypical and neurodivergent development.
